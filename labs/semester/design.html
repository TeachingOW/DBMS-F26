Meetings 3–4: Campus Events case

A department sponsors many events; every event has one sponsor. An event has one room, start/end times, a fee, and a seat capacity. Students register for many events and each registration records time and payment status. Speakers may appear at many events; each speaking assignment has a position within its event. A speaker can appear at most once in an event. Students cannot register twice for the same event. Events in the same room cannot overlap. A registration cannot exceed event capacity.

  1. Extract entities, candidate keys, attributes, relationship attributes, and min/max cardinalities. State whether an event can have zero speakers and whether cancelled registrations remain in history; these are requirements to clarify, not facts to invent silently.
  2. Draw the ER diagram; explain the modeling of Registration and SpeakingAssignment.
  3. Map to relations. Include a composite key for registration and a uniqueness rule for speaker position within an event.
  4. Map five rules to SQL mechanisms. Explain why an ordinary row CHECK cannot count all registrations safely under concurrency.
  5. Exchange models: attempt duplicate registration, orphan event sponsor, duplicate speaker position, inverted times, and overlapping room bookings.
  6. Revise the model and list unresolved requirements separately.

Deliverables: diagram, schemas, five-rule test matrix, and one-page peer-review response. Each studio uses 10 review + 15 explanation + 15 worked example + 50 design/testing + 10 debrief minutes.

Meeting 11: functional dependencies

Enrollment(S, C, T, StudentName, CourseTitle, Instructor, Office, Grade). T identifies a term. Business rules: S → StudentName; C → CourseTitle; CT → Instructor; Instructor → Office; SCT → Grade. Do not infer any other dependency from sample data.

  1. Compute S+, CT+, and SCT+ with explicit steps.
  2. Prove that SCT is the only candidate key under these rules.
  3. Give an update, insertion, and deletion anomaly.
  4. Explain why two students with the same name do not imply StudentName → S.

Meeting 12: decomposition and tradeoffs

  1. Decompose into Student(S, StudentName), Course(C, CourseTitle), InstructorOffice(Instructor, Office), Offering(C,T,Instructor), and EnrollmentGrade(S,C,T,Grade).
  2. Check the determinant of every nontrivial dependency in each relation. Prove losslessness by a sequence of binary decompositions whose intersection determines one side. Verify preservation of every listed FD.
  3. For R(A,B,C) with AB → C and C → B, find all candidate keys. Decide whether R is 3NF and BCNF. Decompose to CB and AC and determine which FD is no longer locally enforceable.
  4. Apply the same audit to your project. Name one business rule not captured by functional dependencies.

Stretch: construct a lossy decomposition of R(A,B,C) into AB and BC with no assumed FDs, showing the spurious tuples after joining.
