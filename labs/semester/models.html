Document models and distributed-system decisions

Meetings 29–30. Build on the existing MongoDB shop lab and MongoDB lecture. Distribution is a short application of existing material, not a new cluster-administration unit.

Meeting 29: modeling and queries

  1. Complete the shop dataset setup and CRUD exercises in the existing lab.
  2. Model an order and its line items once with embedding and once with references. State what happens if a product price changes after checkout.
  3. Write a filter for completed orders and an aggregation by customer using the lab’s actual field names.
  4. Map an order/customer/product relationship back to relational tables. Compare join needs, validation, update frequency, and bounded versus unbounded arrays.
  5. Explain why “schema flexible” does not mean the application has no schema.

Meeting 30: scenarios (100 minutes)

10 review, 15 worked replica-lag example, 60 small-group decisions, 15 defense.

  1. Replica lag: a customer changes an address on the primary at 10:00:00, then reads from a replica two seconds behind. Predict the result and propose read-your-writes behavior.
  2. Last ticket during a partition: two regions each believe one ticket remains and cannot communicate. Compare accepting both requests, rejecting/deferring one region, and reserving disjoint inventory in advance. State the invariant and cost of each choice.
  3. Catalog versus checkout: stale catalog descriptions may be tolerated, but charging twice is not. Specify different consistency/idempotency needs for the two operations.
  4. Analytics: explain why a reporting replica or warehouse can reduce transactional load and what freshness tradeoff follows.
  5. CAP precision: during a network partition, explain the conflict between linearizable consistency and responding to every request. Do not describe CAP as an unconditional “pick any two” menu.

Submit: a two-page decision memo with assumptions, failure outcomes, and reconciliation rules. Stretch: sketch a two-phase-commit coordinator failure and explain blocking without implementing a cluster.
